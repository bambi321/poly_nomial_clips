## Clip 1. Even a "small" nuclear war starves 250 million

- **Time:** 00:06:18.5 → 00:06:51.7 (33s)
- **Opening line (first 3s):** "The most likely small-ish nuclear exchange would be fought today between India and Pakistan…"
- **Payoff:** Even a "mild nuclear autumn" disrupts agriculture enough to starve up to 250 million people worldwide.
- **Why it works:** It flips expectations: the *small* war is the scary one. The stat escalates three times (27 million, more than WWI, 250 million) and lands on the biggest number.
- **Signals:** Sits inside the video's strongest real heat peak (06:02–06:42, peak 0.79), outside the intro, so it's genuine replay interest. Lines are ▅ then ▃.
- **Score:** hook 2.5/3 · stands alone 3/3 · payoff 2/2 · engagement 1.6/2 = 9.1
- **Caption:** see `clips/clip1-caption.txt`
- **Science check:**
  - ~27 million immediate deaths in an India–Pakistan exchange (06:26.5)
  - "more people than all of World War I" (06:34.9)
  - "not a nuclear winter, but a mild nuclear autumn" (06:39.2)
  - "up to 250 million" starve (06:45.3)
  - All stated in the source. These match the published scenario studies Kurzgesagt cites. Keep "up to" and "would" in captions; don't present it as certain.

## Clip 2. 4,400 bombs: the worst case, by the numbers

- **Why it works:** It opens on a huge number and keeps climbing. "Like humanity dropping an asteroid on itself" is the quotable line, and it ends on a clean closing sentence.
- **Signals:** Follows the third heat peak (06:49–07:49). Heat fades from ▃ to ▁ through the clip, so the hook carries it.
- **Score:** hook 3/3 · stands alone 3/3 · payoff 2/2 · engagement 0.5/2 = 8.5
- **Caption:** see `clips/clip2-caption.txt`
- **Science check:**
  - ~4,400 weapons, 360 million immediate deaths (07:44.5)
  - calorie production down "as much as 90%" (07:59.2)

- **Why it works:** It teaches one clear idea: where food comes from, and why a few regions matter so much. Easy to follow without the rest of the video.
- **Signals:** Fourth heat peak (04:08–04:21). ▃ throughout.

## Clip 6. The fire cloud that's deadlier than the mushroom cloud

- **Time:** 00:01:12.6 → 00:01:59.4 (47s)
- **Opening line (first 3s):** "But in the following hours, a far more deadly cloud forms."
- **Payoff:** Above the tropopause there's no weather to wash the soot out, "so it can stay aloft for years."
- **Why it works:** A clear mechanism explainer with a satisfying "so that's why" ending. The opening "But" leans on the caption for context.
- **Signals:** Low heat (▂). Several scene cuts, so the visuals move a lot.
- **Score:** hook 2/3 · stands alone 2.5/3 · payoff 2/2 · engagement 0.4/2 = 6.9
- **Caption:** see `clips/clip6-caption.txt`
- **Science check:**
  - Firestorms create their own wind system and updraft (01:16.8, 01:24.5)
  - A pyrocumulonimbus cloud lifts soot into the stratosphere (01:32.8)
  - Normally soot is washed out by rain, but above the tropopause it can stay for years (01:41.7, 01:53.3)
  - All stated in the source and consistent with standard atmospheric science.
  - Transcript errors here: "tropopores" should be **tropopause**, "pyrocumulinimbus" should be **pyrocumulonimbus**, and "certain aerosols" (01:32.8) is probably "soot and aerosols". Check against the audio.
- **Framing:** diagram
